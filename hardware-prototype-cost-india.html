How much does a hardware prototype cost in India?

What each stage costs, where the money goes, and how to avoid paying for the same work twice.

By Shreyas P, 21 October 2026. 3-minute read.

Short answer: working with an independent engineer, plan on about ₹15,000 for a feasibility study, from ₹50,000 for a proof of concept that works on the bench, and from ₹1.5 lakh for an investor-ready prototype with a custom PCB and enclosure. Parts and fabrication come on top and are usually a small share of the total. Products with motors, many sensors, a cloud app or tight size limits cost more.

Where the money goes

Most of a prototype’s cost is engineering time, not parts. A typical budget splits into:

  • Engineering: architecture, firmware, schematic and PCB design, bring-up and testing. This is the largest part.
  • Components and dev boards: microcontrollers, sensors, radios, power parts, plus spares.
  • PCB fabrication and assembly: small prototype batches of the custom board.
  • Enclosure: usually 3D-printed for a prototype, so it looks like a product.
  • App or dashboard: only if the product needs one to demo.

What makes a prototype cost more

  • More subsystems. Every sensor, radio, motor or display adds design, firmware and testing.
  • Power and size limits. Long battery life or a small enclosure means more design iterations.
  • Connectivity and cloud. Talking to an app or dashboard adds backend, provisioning and security work.
  • Custom hardware. Dev boards are fast for a proof of concept. A custom PCB costs more up front but looks and behaves like a product.
  • Moving parts and tough environments. Mechanisms, sealing and rugged enclosures need more design and testing.
  • Changing requirements. Every change after the design is frozen means rework.

Parts and fabrication: what founders miss

For most prototypes, components, boards and printed enclosures are a modest part of the budget. These are the costs that catch founders out:

  • Spares. Parts get damaged during bring-up. Ordering two or three of everything saves weeks of waiting.
  • Shipping and import duty. On parts and boards ordered from abroad, these can cost more than the parts themselves.
  • A second board revision. The first PCB rarely comes out perfect. Budget for one round of fixes.
  • Enclosure rounds. Printed parts are cheap, but fit and finish usually take a few tries.

What a prototype budget doesn’t include

  • Certification. Approvals such as BIS and WPC in India, or CE for Europe, are needed before you sell, not before you demo. Their cost depends on the product. An investor-ready prototype comes with a certification path, so you know which ones apply.
  • Production tooling. Injection-mould tools and production test fixtures are manufacturing costs.
  • Manufacturing. The Investor Demo Kit estimates unit cost at 100 and 1,000 units, but production is a separate step.

How to keep the cost down

  1. Start with a feasibility sprint. A small fixed fee tells you what’s hard before you commit a large budget.
  2. Prove the riskiest part first. A proof of concept on dev boards is far cheaper than a polished prototype that doesn’t work.
  3. Pick one demo story. Build what investors need to see. Leave the rest for the MVP.
  4. Freeze requirements before the custom PCB. Changes are cheap on paper and expensive on copper.
  5. Use grant funding. Schemes such as the Startup India Seed Fund and Karnataka ELEVATE support prototype development, and milestones can be structured to match your grant.

Red flags in a prototype quote

  • Hourly billing with no cap and no milestones.
  • No NDA before you share the details of your idea.
  • Source code and design files not handed over, or only licensed back to you.
  • Parts marked up with no receipts.
  • A fixed price for the whole product before anyone has checked it can be built.
